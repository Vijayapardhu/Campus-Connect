Privacy Policy

Last updated 5 August 2026

Campus Connect has no servers, no accounts and no analytics, so this policy is mostly a list of things that do not happen. Where something does leave your machine, it is named below.

The short version. The application collects nothing about you and sends nothing to us. There is no “us” to send it to — no backend exists. Your clipboard, messages, files and calls travel directly between your own devices on your own network.

What the application stores

Everything is kept locally, in your operating system’s application-data folder for your user account. None of it is uploaded anywhere.

  • Rooms and their members — names, join codes, and the salt used to derive the room key.
  • Clipboard history and chat — what you shared, kept until it ages out under your retention setting or you clear it.
  • Settings — your device name, theme, shortcuts and preferences.
  • Room keys — cached so you are not asked for the password on every launch.

Deleting the application’s data folder removes all of it. Anyone with access to your user account on your computer can read those files, exactly as they could read any other document you own.

What it never stores

  • Your room passwords. They derive a key and are then discarded.
  • Any identifier that could recognise you across sessions or devices.
  • Usage data, crash reports, feature analytics or telemetry of any kind.

What crosses the network

Within a room, contents are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key derived from that room’s password. Calls and screen sharing are peer to peer, encrypted by DTLS-SRTP.

Somebody watching your local network can still observe that a room is active and roughly how large its messages are, and room and owner names are broadcast openly so devices can find each other at all. A room created without a password is not encrypted, and the application labels it Not encrypted wherever it appears.

When it contacts the internet

Once, and only if you leave it switched on:

  • Update checks. The application asks GitHub whether a newer release exists. That request necessarily tells GitHub your IP address and the time, as any web request would. It carries nothing about you, your rooms or your data, and you can turn it off in Settings → Updates.

There is nothing else. No first-run ping, no licence check, no error reporting.

Your phone

Phone access is off until you switch it on, and pairing requires scanning a code shown on your computer. Since version 0.5.0 that connection is served over TLS, using a certificate your own computer generates and keeps — the private key never leaves that machine.

Your phone will warn you the first time, because no browser has any reason to trust a certificate your laptop issued. The page your phone loads is served by your computer, not by a website, and nothing about that session reaches anybody else.

This website

These pages are static files on GitHub Pages. They set no cookies, run no analytics, and make no requests to any third party — the typefaces are the ones already on your device, and the only external links are ones you can see and choose to click.

GitHub serves the files and, like any host, keeps standard server logs. That is covered by GitHub’s privacy statement and is outside our control.

Children

The application is a local utility that collects no personal information from anyone of any age. Nothing is gathered, so nothing is gathered about children either.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it — and because the page lives in the repository, you can see exactly what was altered and when.

Contact

Questions belong in the issue tracker, in the open, where the answer is useful to the next person who wonders the same thing.
